Pregnant Women Health Care — Arduino Monitor

Arduino · Sensors · IoT · Embedded C · Alert Systems

The problem

Built during the Internet of Things training program with TVS Training and Service, this project targeted a real gap: pregnant women, especially in areas without constant access to a clinic, often can't have their vitals monitored continuously.

What I built

An Arduino-based wearable/bedside monitoring unit that integrates multiple sensors to track vital health parameters — such as heart rate and body temperature — in real time. The system runs a continuous read loop and compares incoming sensor values against safe thresholds.

The alert mechanism

When a reading crosses outside the safe range, the system triggers an alert to notify caregivers immediately, rather than waiting for a scheduled check-in. That shift — from periodic manual checks to continuous automated monitoring — was the core idea behind the project.

What I learned

This was my first hands-on experience combining multiple sensor inputs into one coherent decision system, and it's part of why I'm drawn to data work now: the interesting part wasn't wiring the sensors, it was deciding what the numbers meant and when they mattered enough to act on.
